The investigation originated in generational trauma but converged on a broader correspondence chain: Event → Adaptation → Narrative → Myth → Condition. Trauma became one instance of this process rather than its defining feature. Historical events terminate, adaptations persist, narratives encode adaptations, and myths preserve recurring conditions. Theater localizes those conditions for contemporary audiences, while tragedy functions as a mechanism for producing recognition. Recognition transforms observation into self-reference, enabling transformation. The strongest surviving formulation is that myths persist not because events are remembered, but because the conditions they encode continue to recur. Relevant correspondences emerged through cross-domain comparison among trauma, Greek tragedy, Odysseus, Frazerian reconstruction, and La Llorona discussions.
