## Relational Ontology: Locality, Liberty, and TAN

### Context

The discussion originated in symbolic editing, trailer grammar, serialization, topology, Go liberties, and manifold structure.

The initial goal was not ontology.

The initial goal was understanding why certain structures repeatedly survived abstraction while others repeatedly collapsed.

The investigation progressively detached from:

- editing
- narrative
- representation

and converged toward:

- locality
- liberty
- continuity
- realization
- topology
- Yang/Yin
- Mu/Taiji
- TAN

The resulting notebook is not fundamentally about topology.

It is about the relationship between possibility, locality, continuity, and the apparent existence of stable objects.

---

### Starting Position

Several assumptions appeared reasonable:

- State is fundamental.
- Continuity is path history.
- Yang opposes Yin.
- TAN is an object.
- Objects precede relationships.
- Realization creates value.

All survived partially.

None survived unchanged.

---

### Evolution I

#### Question

What is state?

#### Exploration

State was examined through Go liberties, possibility spaces, and realization.

#### Observations

Realized states consistently appeared to emerge from reductions in possibility.

The more realized a position became, the fewer liberties remained.

#### Outcome

State = Collapsed Liberty

State became derived rather than fundamental.

---

### Evolution II

#### Question

What survives abstraction?

#### Exploration

Paths, traversal, continuity, and topology were compared.

#### Observations

Specific paths disappeared under abstraction.

Accessibility remained.

#### Outcome

Continuity ≠ Path History

Continuity = Preserved Accessibility

---

### Evolution III

#### Question

How should Yang and Yin be interpreted?

#### Exploration

Traditional polarity models were stress-tested against locality analysis and earlier Mu/Taiji work.

#### Observations

Yang repeatedly behaved as local organization.

Yin repeatedly behaved as continuity.

Opposition lost explanatory power.

#### Outcome

Yang ⊂ Yin

Yang = Localized Relevance

---

### Evolution IV

#### Question

What is realization?

#### Exploration

Possibility and constraint were examined together.

#### Observations

Realization consistently removed alternatives.

It never created value.

#### Outcome

Realization = Constraint

Realization = Reduction of alternatives

---

### Evolution V

#### Question

What is TAN?

#### Exploration

TAN was modeled successively as:

- Axis
- Transport
- Interface

#### Observations

Each formulation required external definitions.

None survived independently.

#### Outcome

TAN lost objecthood.

---

### Evolution VI

#### Question

If TAN is not an object, what is it?

#### Exploration

Relationship-first formulations were examined.

#### Observations

Every TAN formulation required:

Locus

and

Orbit

to exist.

TAN never appeared independently.

#### Outcome

TAN(X,Z)

where:

X = Locus

Z = Orbit

TAN = Relationship

---

### Evolution VII

#### Question

What is more fundamental:
objects or relationships?

#### Exploration

State, continuity, Yang, and TAN were re-examined.

#### Observations

Every surviving structure appeared to be derived from deeper relationships.

Objects repeatedly became effects.

Relationships repeatedly became causes.

#### Outcome

Object
→ Relationship

became:

Relationship
→ Apparent Object
