##### Title

Editing as Relationship Architecture

###### Abstract

A practical editing exercise evolved into an ontology of meaning construction.

Footage, references, cards, transitions, and assets progressively lost explanatory primacy while serialization, relationships, implication, escalation, and audience participation became increasingly central.

The notebook ultimately reclassifies editing from asset management into relationship architecture.

Meaning emerges not from footage itself, but from relationships established between symbols and realized through audience interpretation.

Narrative emerges as transmitted structure.

Story emerges as constructed interpretation.

Editing becomes the deliberate arrangement of relationships that enable both.

A practical editing exercise evolved into an ontology of meaning construction. Assets proved replaceable while relationships proved durable. Serialization generates relationships, relationships generate interpretation, interpretation enables narrative, narrative enables story construction, and story produces meaning. Editing was ultimately redefined as relationship architecture. The notebook's central contribution is the discovery that meaning emerges between symbols and is realized through audience participation rather than direct transmission.
